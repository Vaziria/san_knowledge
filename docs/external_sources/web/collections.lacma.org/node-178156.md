# Kendi with Hanuman

- **Artist or Maker:** Unknown
- **Date:** circa 14th century
- **Place Made:** Indonesia, Eastern Java
- **Medium:** Earthenware
- **Dimensions:** 11 x 5 x 6 1/4 in. (27.94 x 12.7 x 15.88 cm)
- **Credit Line:** Gift of Daniel Ostroff
- **Accession Number:** M.90.180.2
- **Classification:** Furnishings
- **Collecting Area:** South and Southeast Asian Art

This earthenware kendi, a pouring vessel common across Southeast Asia, has its upper portion shaped as the head of Hanuman, the divine monkey general from the Indian epics Ramayana and Mahabharata. The flattened cylinder on his head is the rim for a lid that is now missing. The figure has wide eyes, a mustache, and bared fangs, and wears a necklace of round ornaments. The spout takes the form of two joined monkey faces.
