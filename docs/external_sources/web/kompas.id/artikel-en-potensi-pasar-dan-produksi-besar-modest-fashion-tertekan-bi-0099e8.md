# Muslim Fashion Sales Dim During Ramadan and Eid 2026

JAKARTA, KOMPAS — The momentum of Ramadan and Idul Fitri boosts the sales of Muslim clothing compared to regular days. However, during the Ramadan-Eid period of 2026, sales have declined compared to the same period in previous years.

The Chairperson of the Indonesian Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises Industry Association (Akumandiri), Hermawati Setyorinny, stated that the sales of Muslim clothing during Ramadan and Eid 2026 continue to increase compared to regular days. However, the growth is not as high as in the last two to three years.

Muslim fashion is a style of clothing that adheres to Islamic principles. Sales typically increase during the Ramadan-Eid al-Fitr period compared to normal days.

"Ramadan and Eid are certainly moments awaited by MSME actors because sales can increase several times over. However, this time it is not as euphoric as in the previous two seasons. Now, an increase of around 50 percent is already considered good. In the past, it could reach up to double or around 200 percent," said Hermawati when contacted on Sunday (22/3/2026).

The indications of this slowdown, Hermawati continued, are evident in fashion trade centers such as Tanah Abang and Thamrin City, which are not as crowded as during Ramadan and Eid in previous years. Shopping activities are still present, but do not show a significant increase.

Changes in consumer behavior have become one of the main factors. Amid rising price pressures, the public tends to shift their spending towards more prioritized needs, particularly food and beverages during Ramadan. There are holiday discounts, but they are not strong enough to boost sales significantly.

Structurally, the challenges of the Muslim fashion industry arise not only from the demand side but also from the still high production costs. The prices of domestic textile raw materials, which are subject to value-added tax (VAT), make local products less competitive compared to imported goods.

"If purchasing materials or textile products domestically is actually more expensive than importing, this makes our products less competitive," said Hermawati.

## Chinese Products

Fashion products, including Muslim clothing from Indonesia, must compete with products from China. Chinese products are increasingly dominating the market due to production efficiency and lower prices.

"Make no mistake, China produces a lot of clothing, Muslim fashion, not just for women, but also for men," he said.

This condition not only pressures fashion MSME actors but also impacts the overall structure of the national textile and textile products (TPT) industry. The influx of imported products in large quantities narrows the space for local products in the domestic market, which is actually very promising.

Indeed, according to Hermawati, Indonesia is the country with the largest Muslim population in the world. "We are the largest Muslim community, this presents an opportunity for the domestic industry, including small and medium enterprises, to dominate the market. However, domestic products are struggling to compete," she said.

Global trends also indicate that Muslim fashion is increasingly in demand, thereby opening opportunities for expansion into international markets. The uniqueness of designs that incorporate elements of local culture adds value that has yet to be fully optimized.

However, this opportunity is still hindered by various factors, ranging from high production costs, limited price competitiveness, to uneven access to export markets. Many MSME actors still require assistance to penetrate the global market, both in terms of product standards, marketing, and distribution networks.

In this context, Hermawati assesses that the momentum of Eid remains an important driver for the Muslim fashion industry. However, this is more seasonal in nature.

Without strengthening from upstream to downstream, including policies that support production efficiency and the protection of the domestic market, the surge in demand during holidays risks being insufficient to drive sustainable growth.

"With a large domestic market and open global potential, Indonesia is considered to have significant opportunities and potential to become a center for modest fashion production both domestically and globally. The realization of this potential depends on the ability to strengthen the competitiveness of the domestic industry," he said.

The Minister of Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (UMKM), Maman Abdurrahman, in his press release, emphasized the importance of maintaining and optimizing Indonesia's vast domestic market potential to prevent it from being dominated by imported products, while also continuing to encourage the strengthening of superior products from UMKM entrepreneurs to penetrate the export market.

"Without undermining efforts to encourage superior MSME products to penetrate the export market, our domestic market must be well maintained. With a population of around 250 million people, our market potential is very large. If it were not significant, it would be unlikely that our domestic market would become a target for various imported products," said Maman.

Maman stated that the government continues to strengthen various strategic measures to maintain the potential of the domestic market while also protecting small and medium enterprises from unhealthy competition.

One of the steps taken is to regulate the circulation of illegal imported goods to ensure the sustainability of domestic MSME businesses.

## Export market

According to the State of the Global Islamic Economy (SGIE) Report 2024/2025, the Muslim fashion market is projected to reach 91.93 billion US dollars (Rp 1,379 trillion) in 2024 and 96.8 billion US dollars (Rp 1,452 trillion) in 2025.

By 2033, its value is even projected to reach 146.37 billion US dollars (approximately Rp 2,195 trillion) with an average growth rate of around 5.3 percent per year. In Indonesia, the market value of Muslim fashion is estimated to reach Rp 300 trillion per year.

Minister of Industry Agus Gumiwang Kartasasmita stated that global consumption of Muslim fashion products continues to increase. "This shows that the modest fashion industry has enormous market potential and must grow to create strategic opportunities for Indonesia to strengthen its role as a producer and global center of modest fashion trends," Agus said in his statement.

![Fashion show by Indonesian designers at the Istanbul Fashion Connection (IFCO) 2025 event, Thursday (21/8/2025), in Istanbul, Türkiye.](https://assetd.kompas.id/IcFDAOnMDF-sxEL76VsJaDl7VI0=/1024x576/smart/filters:format%28webp%29:quality%2880%29/https://cdn-dam.kompas.id/images/2025/08/22/27784abea165599a1e19af19933ea71b-cropped_image.jpg)

The domestic Muslim fashion industry's potential in the global market is also very promising. Indonesian Muslim fashion exports to member countries of the Organization of Islamic Cooperation (OIC) are projected to reach US$990 million in 2023.

Compared to 2022, the value increased by 83 percent in 2022. At that time, its exports were valued at 540 million US dollars. Nevertheless, China still dominates the export of fashion products to OIC member countries. Following are Turkey and India.

"It is the responsibility of all stakeholders to enhance Indonesia's role in the global Muslim fashion market," Agus said.

The Director General of Small, Medium, and Miscellaneous Industries (IKMA), Reni Yanita, stated that Indonesia has great potential to become a global center for Muslim fashion. This is supported by the creativity of local designers, cultural richness, and the diversity of high-quality textile materials that form the nation's identity.

In line with the implementation of the national halal certification obligation, Reni continued, halal certification will also begin to be applied to Muslim clothing starting in October 2026.

"This policy not only guarantees quality, safety, and consumer confidence, but also creates added value for Indonesian modest fashion products in the global market," Reni said.

Indonesia, as a country that comprehensively implements halal certification on consumer goods, can provide differentiation and competitive advantages compared to products from other countries, he continued.

Through guaranteed and integrated halal standards, Reni added, Indonesian Muslim fashion products have a greater opportunity to penetrate the export market, especially countries with large Muslim populations and the global market which increasingly prioritizes aspects of ethical fashion, sustainability, and product traceability.
