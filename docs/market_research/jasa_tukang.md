# Jasa Tukang Market Research

Market research on Indonesia's home repair, renovation and handyman (*jasa tukang*) market, done 2026-09-17 to decide which product to build. It covers market size and demand, competitors and pricing. Most figures come from news, blogs and paid-research teasers; BPS and APJII figures quoted through news are marked **(via news)**. Product ideas at the end are a recommendation, not a decision.

## Summary

- **Nobody sizes the services market.** Published "home improvement" figures (about USD 0.75–1.67B) count product retail only, not tukang labour. A service-market size has to be built bottom-up, and the inputs for that (renovation frequency, spend per job) are not published.
- **Demand is large:** about 75M households, about 87% own their home, and 18.01M owned homes are not adequately liveable (BPS 2026, via news).
- **Supply is large and informal:** 8.7M construction workers (BPS Feb 2025, via news).
- **Trust is the core problem:** tukang taking the down payment and disappearing, unfinished work, no-shows. Evidence is news cases and app reviews, not surveys.
- **Apps cost about 1.6–1.8× the BPS wage median** and offer 14–60 day warranties. Providers resent platform fees (Sejasa partners claim 35%).
- **Generalist home-service apps have failed** (Gojek GoLife/GoFix closed in 2020). Kanggo, the strongest managed player, is Jabodetabek only and gets real traction from B2B clients.
- **Headwind:** the middle class shrank from 57.33M (2019) to 46.7M people (2025) (BPS, via news).

### Demand drivers

- **Households:** about 75M, derived from BPS backlog ratios (9.29M = 12.39%).
- **Home ownership:** about 87–88% of households own their home (BPS Statistik Perumahan 2026, via [pkp.go.id](../external_sources/web/pkp.go.id/berita-detail-bps-catat-backlog-kepemilikan-dan-kelayakhunian-rumah-te-34b5a1.md)).
- **Ownership backlog:** 12.75M (2020) → 9.29M households (2026) (BPS, via [Bisnis](../external_sources/web/ekonomi.bisnis.com/read-20260806-47-1994161-bps-ungkap-backlog-perumahan-ri-turun-346-jut-0ab766.md)).
- **Liveability backlog** (owned but not adequately liveable), the most direct repair driver: 18.77M (2025) → 18.01M households (2026), largest in West, Central and East Java (BPS, via news).
- **Urbanisation:** 58.8% urban (2024); World Bank expects more than 70% by 2045.
- **Middle class:** 57.33M (2019) → 46.7M people (2025), 16.6% of the population (BPS, via Kontan). Discretionary renovation spend is under pressure.
- **Housing stock age:** no data found.

### Supply

- **Construction workers:** more than 8.7M, 5.97% of the employed (BPS Sakernas Feb 2025, via [Katadata](../external_sources/web/katadata.co.id/finansial-makro-68a423af1f2fb-bps-sektor-konstruksi-serap-8-7-juta-pek-62e4cc.md)).
- **Informality:** no construction-specific figure. Proxy: 7.37M non-farm casual workers (Aug 2023, BPS definition via a secondary site).

### How customers find tukang

- **No quantitative data found.** Word of mouth, neighbours and building-supply shop (*toko bangunan*) referrals are described as the main channel, followed by WhatsApp, Facebook groups, OLX and apps.
- **Internet penetration:** 80.66%, 229.4M users, up from 79.50% in 2024; Java 84.69% (APJII 2025, via [Antara](../external_sources/web/antaranews.com/berita-5019229-apjii-catat-tingkat-penetrasi-internet-indonesia-capai-461987.md)).

### Gaps

1. **Trust and deposit fraud:** the typical scam asks for about 50% down and disappears; one reported case lost about Rp 85M. Sejasa's anti-fraud cover is capped at Rp 20M.
2. **No-shows and slow response** are the top app complaints for both Kanggo and Sejasa.
3. **Price transparency:** disputes over AC and freon pricing; fixed-price menus are the current answer.
4. **Outside Jabodetabek:** the managed players are Jakarta (or Surabaya) only; other cities rely on OLX, Facebook and small local apps.
5. **Supply-side unhappiness:** providers resent lead and credit fees. A fairer deal could win the best tukang.
6. **B2B maintenance** (property managers, retail chains, *kos* owners) is proven in Jakarta (Kanggo, Seekmi's pivot) and looks open elsewhere.

## Product Options

Candidates drawn from the gaps above. None is validated yet.

| Option | Customer | Why it could work | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Maintenance for small property owners** outside Jabodetabek (e.g. Surabaya or Bandung) | *Kos* owners, small landlords, shop/cafe chains | Repeat jobs and recurring revenue; Kanggo proves B2B demand in Jakarta; cheaper to acquire than consumers; less exposed to middle-class squeeze | Needs a reliable tukang bench in one city before scaling |
| **B. Trust layer for renovation jobs** | Homeowners and tukang/small contractors | Directly addresses deposit fraud: written quote (RAB), staged escrow payments, photo progress, warranty | Payments/escrow licensing; hard to force both sides onto it |
| **C. Fixed-price small jobs, fair to tukang** | Urban households | Clear prices + low commission (5–10%) to win the best tukang from Sejasa | Crowded, high marketing cost; the GoLife pattern |
| **D. Tools for tukang** (quotes, invoices, job booking via WhatsApp) | Tukang and mandor | 8.7M workers, no incumbent tool | Low willingness to pay |

**Recommendation:** start with **A** in one city, borrowing B's escrow and warranty as the trust feature. It avoids the crowded consumer fight in Jakarta, builds repeat revenue, and grows a vetted tukang bench that C or D can use later.
