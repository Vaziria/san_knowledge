# Jasa Tukang Market: Blitar

Research (2026-09-17) on Blitar, East Java, as a target area for the home repair and maintenance business described in [the national jasa tukang research](jasa_tukang.md). It covers Kota Blitar (the city) and Kabupaten Blitar (the surrounding regency). Several BPS and news pages blocked downloads, so some figures come from search snippets and are marked **(snippet)**; BPS figures read through news are marked **(via news)**.

## Summary

- **Small but reachable:** Kota Blitar has about 162k people, and the regency about 1.27M spread over a rural area; together about 1.43M.
- **Low price level:** 2026 minimum wage (UMK) is about Rp 2.6M a month, and a tukang earns Rp 100–150k a day, around the East Java BPS median of Rp 126k and below the Rp 140k national median. Little room for a platform commission.
- **No national player is active:** Sejasa has 0 AC providers and 6 barely reviewed tukang providers in Blitar; Kanggo, Tukang.com and Pinhome do not serve it.
- **Local competition is informal:** about 10 Google-rated AC shops, a WhatsApp lead site (tukangblitar.com), Facebook groups.
- **Distinctive niche:** Kabupaten Blitar registered 5,376 migrant workers (PMI) in 2025, mostly women in Hong Kong and Taiwan. A 2023 village study found almost all women migrants leave mainly to build or renovate a house. The number of families renovating each year is still unknown.
- **Verdict:** too small for an app or marketplace; could work as a **local, WhatsApp-first managed service** or as a pilot city for an East Java cluster.

## Housing and Migrant Workers

- **Unfit homes (RTLH), Kabupaten:** 5,653 left at the end of 2025. For 2026 the regency funds 400 repairs and the national BSPS programme 987 units, 276 under way by July ([Persada Blitar](../external_sources/web/persadablitar.id/kabupaten-blitar-kebagian-987-unit-bsps-tahun-2026-percepatan-perbaika-487858.md)).
- **Unfit homes, Kota:** 837 units; BSPS covers 744 in 2026 at Rp 20M each (Rp 17.5M materials, Rp 2.5M labour), worked on 10 Jul–10 Oct 2026 (Kompas, snippet).
- These programmes are self-help and one-off, so they are **not a paying market**, and they keep local tukang busy in the second half of 2026.
- **Kos:** Mamikos lists only about 14–51 kos per category, rooms Rp 200–500k a month (Rp 700k+ with AC). The rental stock looks small and low-rent, which weakens the kos-owner idea from the national research.
- **Migrant workers (PMI), Kabupaten Blitar** (regency labour office):
  - 4,446 (2022) and 4,167 (2023), most from Ponggok, Kademangan and Gandusari districts ([detik, Jan 2024](../external_sources/web/detik.com/jatim-berita-d-7170498-4-ribu-warga-blitar-jadi-pekerja-migran-terbany-3ce646.md)).
  - 5,376 registered in E-PMI in 2025: Hong Kong 3,454, Taiwan 1,223, Japan 205, South Korea 175, Malaysia 84 ([Persada Blitar, Sep 2025](../external_sources/web/persadablitar.id/5-376-pmi-asal-kabupaten-blitar-telah-terdata-melalui-e-pmi-hongkong-t-ab3111.md)).
  - 2,726 served by BP3MI Jatim in Jan–Nov 2025, 3rd in East Java after Ponorogo and Tulungagung ([Harian Bhirawa, Dec 2025](../external_sources/web/harianbhirawa.co.id/bp3mi-jatim-perkuat-layanan-dan-evaluasi-penempatan-pmi-sepanjang-2025.md)).
- **National rank** (BP2MI via news; counts "Blitar" without splitting Kota and Kabupaten, so they run about twice the regency's own figures): 7th with 8,634 in 2023 (Databoks), 5th with 9,952 in Jan–Nov 2024 (Kompas, snippet).
- **Kota Blitar:** only 59 departures in Jan–Mar 2026 (city labour office, snippet). The migrant-worker niche is a regency market, not a city one.
- **Remittances into housing:** no remittance value for Blitar or East Java (national: Rp 253.3T in 2024, Rp 288T in 2025, Bank Indonesia via Kompas). But a [2023 study of Desa Kolomayan, Wonodadi](../external_sources/web/ejournal.unikama.ac.id/index-php-jpig-article-download-8503-4070.md) (Khuriyah & Purnomo, JPIG 8(2); 65 of 189 women migrant workers surveyed) found:
  - 38% of the village's 729 households are families of women migrant workers; 60% of its workforce has worked abroad.
  - Almost all women migrants leave mainly to raise money to **build or renovate a house**.
  - First remittances go to building a house (37%), buying land (24%) and paying debts (14%).
  - 90% of migrant families' homes now meet the liveable-house standard, often built "more luxurious" for prestige.
  - 57.6% start a business after returning (small shops, food, services, poultry, online shops).
  - Pay is Rp 6M+ a month in Hong Kong and Taiwan; leaving costs Rp 15–25M, usually repaid by 6–9 months of salary deductions.
- **Migrant villages:** Kolomayan (Wonodadi); Desmigratif programme villages Plosorejo (Kademangan) and Siraman (Kesamben).

## Competition

- **National platforms:** Sejasa's Blitar pages show 0 AC providers and [6 tukang providers](../external_sources/web/sejasa.com/mitra-kami-jasa-tukang-bangunan-jawa-timur-blitar.md) with 0–17 reviews each. Kanggo and Tukang.com are Jabodetabek only; Pinhome covers large cities only.
- **Local AC shops:** at least 10 rated 4.5–5.0 on Google Maps, e.g. Eko AC, CV Bang AC, Jaya Mandiri, ABY Jaya ([list](../external_sources/web/tokoterdekat.dapodik.co.id/service-ac-blitar.md)). None publish prices.
- **Informal:** tukangblitar.com (WhatsApp lead site, claims 60+ services), Dadang Teknik on Facebook, urgent.id listings, Facebook groups such as "Jual Beli area Blitar".
- **No local home-service app** found. OLX listing counts not checked.

## Assessment

**Too small for a platform.** The urban market is about the size of one large Jakarta district, incomes and wages are low, and national apps have not bothered to build supply here.

**What could work:** a local, WhatsApp-first managed service based in Kota Blitar, reaching nearby districts (Kanigoro, Garum, Nglegok, Sanankulon):

1. **Fixed-price menu** for AC cleaning, electrical, plumbing and small repairs, with a bench of 5–10 vetted tukang.
2. **Maintenance subscription** for shops, clinics, UMKM, homestays and egg farms.
3. **Remote renovation for migrant-worker families:** the worker abroad pays in stages and gets photo and video progress, which answers the deposit-fraud problem. This is the most distinctive angle: a village study confirms housing is the main reason women migrate, but how many families renovate each year, and whether they would pay for oversight, is still untested. Start in the high-PMI districts (Ponggok, Kademangan, Gandusari, Wonodadi).

Later, expand to Tulungagung, Kediri and Malang as an East Java cluster.

**Risks:**

- Customers are price-sensitive, and informal tukang found by word of mouth are cheaper.
- The kos stock is small and low-rent.
- Migrant-remittance renovation demand is supported by one village study but not measured across the regency, and renovations may cluster years apart.
- Government home-repair programmes absorb local labour.
- AC service is already covered by established shops.
- The addressable market is small, so Blitar works best as a pilot, not a standalone business.

## Open Questions
