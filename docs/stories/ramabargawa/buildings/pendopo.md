# Pendopo

Research on the pendopo (pendhapa, pendapa), the open pavilion of Javanese architecture, for the pendopo of Pertapan Jatisrana. The model it informs is `project/bargawa/blender/buildings/pendopo.blend` (built by `scripts/buildings/pendopo.py`, shape in `scripts/common/joglo.py`); this doc has the same name and place in the docs.

Facts come from the sources listed at the end and are marked where the sources disagree or are weak. The last part, "Our pendopo", compares the model with the research and proposes changes; those are proposals, not decisions.

## What a pendopo is

- **A large open pavilion on columns**, square or rectangular in plan, open on all sides: it shades from sun and rain and lets the breeze and soft light through ([Wikipedia: Pendhapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendhapa)). Multatuli's *Max Havelaar* (1860) called it "certainly the most simple representation of the idea 'roof'".
- **The name**: Javanese *pendhapa*; Indonesian *pendapa*; *pendopo* is the common non-standard spelling. It is usually traced to the Sanskrit *mandapa*, "hall" or "pavilion" (Wikipedia marks this as needing a citation). In Indian temples a mandapa is a pavilion in front of the sacred room.
- **Its place in the house**: the pendopo is the **front-most** building of a traditional Javanese house (*omah*), open to guests; the private house lies behind it.
  - The ideal noble compound runs from the front: regol (gate), pendopo, pringgitan, emperan, dalem (the main house with its senthong, inner rooms), gandok (side wings), pawon (kitchen) ([Wikipedia: Javanese traditional house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_traditional_house)).
  - Between the pendopo and the pringgitan runs the *longkangan*, a passage once used by carriages ([Adianti](https://www.ejournal.widyamataram.ac.id/index.php/pendapa/article/view/66/52)).
  - The **pringgitan** is the space between the pendopo and the dalem where wayang is performed. It is named after *ringgit*, wayang.
- A pendopo can also **stand alone**: the pendopo of a kabupaten (regency) faces its alun-alun (town square), and palaces have great pendopo for audiences and ceremonies.

## History

- **The oldest images** of Javanese vernacular buildings are in the **Borobudur reliefs** (9th century), and include a stepped-roof pavilion of the pendhapa type. These buildings sheltered law courts, clergy, palaces, and the king's public appearances.
- **Archaeology** shows square raised bases with **umpak** (stone column bases) and holes for wooden pillars:
  - at Ratu Boko near Prambanan (9th century)
  - at Trowulan, the Majapahit capital (14th century, brick bases)

  The wooden superstructures have not survived.
- Later examples: Kraton Kasepuhan, Cirebon (16th century) and Kotagede, Yogyakarta (17th century), both in the Majapahit brick-base style. From the 18th century European elements entered palace pendopo. Most pendopo are timber; masonry ones exist (Kraton Kanoman, Cirebon).
- Wikipedia notes that the form changed little over more than a thousand years. Its history section cites no sources, so take these dates as indicative.

## What it is used for

- **Receiving guests** and **gathering** family, relatives and neighbours. It expresses harmony (*kerukunan*) between the household and the community ([Dimensi, Petra](https://dimensi.petra.ac.id/index.php/ars/article/view/15706)).
- **Ceremonies** and meetings of nobles; in old Java, **law courts and royal audiences**.
- **Wayang, gamelan and dance**: wayang is played between the pendopo and the pringgitan, and the audience sits in the pendopo ([Adianti](https://www.ejournal.widyamataram.ac.id/index.php/pendapa/article/view/66/52)).
- Work: cottage industries, batik.
- Today: weddings, receptions, cultural events. The use has shifted, but the pendopo is still valued and needed.

## Form and structure

### Roof types and status

The roof tells the owner's rank ([Wikipedia: Javanese traditional house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_traditional_house); [Wikipedia: Joglo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joglo)):

| Roof | Who |
|---|---|
| Kampung | common people |
| Limasan | higher-status families; like a joglo but without tumpang sari |
| **Joglo** | nobility, palaces, official residences; the most complex Javanese roof |
| Tajug (meru) | sacred buildings: mosques, temples |

A pendopo of standing is a **joglo**:
- The columns get taller towards the centre.
- The four inner columns (soko guru) are the tallest and carry the steepest roof of any Javanese type: nearly a pyramid, with a short ridge.
- Unlike the kampung and limasan, a joglo uses **no king posts**.

### The roof's layers, top to bottom

([GNFI: struktur atap joglo](https://www.goodnewsfromindonesia.id/2026/07/27/mengenal-struktur-atap-rumah-joglo-dari-brunjung-hingga-emper-yang-berurutan-dari-atas-ke-bawah))
1. **Brunjung**: the top, the steepest, directly over the soko guru: the building's peak.
2. **Penanggap**: below it, a limasan-shaped roof round the peak; it helps warm air rise and keeps the inside cool.
3. **Penitih**: below that, linking the upper roof to the layers beneath.
4. **Paningrat**: a decorated projection at the centre front.
5. **Emper**: the outermost, gentlest roof, shading the edge; the transition between inside and outside.

Smaller joglo have fewer layers. **Joglo Lawakan** has a simple two-tier roof ([GNFI](https://www.goodnewsfromindonesia.id/2025/02/07/keunikan-jenis-atap-rumah-joglo-dan-fungsinya-dalam-arsitektur-tradisional)).

### Types of joglo

The sources disagree, so this only shows that many named types exist:
- Wikipedia: Kepuhan (used for the pringgitan), Taraju Mas, Lambang Gantung or Pangrawit (the royal audience hall), Wantah, Ceblokan, Tawon Boni and Semar Tinandu, each in a larger male and a smaller female version.
- Popular sources (GNFI, Gramedia) add others:
  - Lawakan: two tiers, simple
  - Sinom: 36 posts, 4 of them soko guru
  - Mangkurat: three tiers, the middle higher
  - Hageng: the largest, with a surrounding *tratak*
  - Pangrawit: with *lambang gantung*

  Some of these sources list Semar Tinandu as a limasan instead.

### Pakem soko: the sizes of the posts

How big the soko and their umpak should be. Researched at the user's request ("Pakem Soko Pendopo"), 2026-10-18.

#### The rules of the Kawruh Kalang

The *Kawruh Kalang* (with the *Kawruh Griya*, Javanese building texts from the turn of the 19th to the 20th century), as read by [Prijotomo (2005), "Pengkonstruksian sektor guru dari griya Jawa: tafsir atas Kawruh Kalang", *Dimensi* 33(1/2): 99–111](https://dimensi.petra.ac.id/index.php/ars/article/view/16350), Table 1.

- **Everything is measured from the blandar–pengeret** (the frame of beams on the soko guru, called *pamidhangan* or *midhangan* in a joglo, "the shoulders"). That is why those beams are called *guru*, the reference: "not by guessing" (*boten kenging … saking dugi-dugi kemawon*). Fix the blandar and pengeret, and the whole building's size follows.
- **The petungan (counting) for the blandar and pengeret**:
  - Count their length in *kaki* through the cycle **1 Sri (Sari), 2 Kitri, 3 Gana, 4 Liyu (Layu), 5 Pokah (Bakah)**, repeating.
  - A **joglo** must land on **Kitri** (2, 7, 12, 17, 22, 27 kaki), a limasan on Sri, a tajug or kampung on Gana.
  - The paper does not define the *kaki* unit.
- **Soko guru length = the diagonal of the blandar–pengeret frame**: "the length of the saka is taken from the diagonal inside the frame".
- **Umpak under the soko guru**: its height = the **perimeter of the soko guru's section**.
- **Umpak under the soko penanggap**: its height = **half the perimeter** of the soko penanggap's section.
- **Umpak under the soko emper**: "as fits", but never as tall as the others.
- **Sunduk pamanjang** (the long tie): as long as the blandar, as thick as the midhangan, its other side ¾ of that. The **sunduk panyelak** (the short tie): as long as the pengeret, the same section, set at a slant with a purus. The **sunduk kili**: like the midhangan pamanjang.
- **Molo** (ridge beam): square in section and **larger than the soko guru**, "so that it is the biggest of all the frame".
- **Dudur** (hip rafter): the frame's diagonal plus the depth of all the tumpang.
- **Gonja** is an extra length at the top of the soko guru, like a capital. It lifts the frame higher, and then a **santen** (a short connecting post) is needed between the blandar–pengeret and the sunduk.

#### A real Joglo Lawakan, measured

The **Pendopo Agung Tamansiswa**, Yogyakarta: foundation 10 July 1938, inaugurated 16 November 1938. It is a **Joglo Lawakan Lambang Teplok** with clay tile roofing, 16.55 × 15.55 m ([Jogjacagar, the DIY cultural heritage register](https://jogjacagar.jogjaprov.go.id/detail/1532/pendopo-agung)):

| Soko | Count | Section | Height | Umpak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| soko guru | 4 | 22 × 22 cm | 6.25 m | 70 × 70 × 80 cm |
| soko penanggap | 12 | 15 × 15 cm | 3.75 m | 32 × 32 × 30 cm |
| soko emper (north and south porches) | 7 per side | 10 × 10 cm | 2.65 m | 28 × 28 × 16 cm |

Spacing: soko guru 6 m (north-south) × 5 m (east-west), soko guru to soko penanggap 5 m. The floor is 60 cm above the yard.

**The umpak rule holds here**: 22 cm posts have an 88 cm perimeter and an 80 cm umpak; 15 cm posts give ½ × 60 = 30 cm, and the umpak is exactly 30 cm. The soko guru length rule does not hold literally: the 6 × 5 m frame's diagonal is 7.8 m against 6.25 m posts.

**Other examples**:
- Pesanggrahan Karang Kemuning (a small pavilion): soko guru 13.5 × 13.5 cm, 2.88 m including the umpak ([Jogjacagar](https://jogjacagar.jogjaprov.go.id/detail/2599/pesanggrahan-karang-kemuning)).
- Sellers' joglo pendopo, a weaker guide: soko guru 20–40 cm in section, main posts about 4 m, for buildings from 8 × 8 to 12 × 10.5 m. One offers soko guru 40 cm with "soko peracik" 20 cm on 12 × 10.5 m ([Threads](https://www.threads.com/@rumahjoglo_rumahkayujati/post/DIqEuMWTUGr/video-pendopo-joglo-soko-guru-40cm-soko-peracik-20cm-ukuran-bangunan-12-meter-x-105-me), [Swasana Jati](https://swasanajati.id/product/pendopo-joglo-minimalis-jati-pd-jg003/)).

**Measured proportions** ([Novianto et al., ARDC 2025, Atlantis Press](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/126022866.pdf); photo-based, on joglo along the Pantura road): the heights of brunjung : penanggap : saka were 2 : 1 : 3 in three houses, 1 : 0.8 : 3 and 2 : 1 : 4 in two each. The paper's introduction also mentions a "2:1 ratio" of saka guru height to pendapa width and a "traditional 3:2 penanggap ratio", without saying how these are measured.

What changed and why:
- **The umpak rule is followed exactly**, as Tamansiswa follows it.
- **The posts are slimmer**, as the proposal asked (about 20 and 14 cm). They stay a little sturdier than Tamansiswa's 1 : 28, which scaled strictly would give posts of about 14 cm; the heavier posts suit the stylized look and still read from the story's camera distances.
- **The soko penanggap ring moved out**, from ±4.0 to ±4.7 m, and the soko penanggap are lower (top 2.45 m above the floor). That gives Tamansiswa's height relation and its spacing, and the penanggap roof keeps its pitch: its eave is now 2.0 m above the floor, at a half-size of 6.0 m (0.8 m past the plinth).
- **The literal "soko guru = frame diagonal" rule was not used.** A 4.5 m soko guru on its 0.72 m pedestal would lift the centre and the whole roof by about 1.2 m, a tower over a small 3.2 m frame, and Tamansiswa, a real joglo, does not follow it either. The centre keeps its 4.0 m height above the floor, the tumpang sari and roof unchanged above it.

### Earthquake behaviour

Java lies in a strong earthquake zone. The **rong-rongan**, the core of umpak, soko guru and tumpang sari, works as a "core in frame" ([Kusumaningayu, Tjendani & Muzaiyana, 2023](https://jurnal.ukdc.ac.id/index.php/LKR/article/download/339/296), quoting Mainah 2017):
- The umpak lessens the shaking that reaches the posts.
- The sunduk and kili stiffen the frame.
- The tumpang sari carries the rafters.

Model tests cited there found the rong-rongan stable under earthquakes of high frequency and low to medium acceleration.

### Materials

- **Teak** (jati) for posts and beams; Mangkunegaran's came from its own forest.
- **Stone** for the umpak and plinth.
- Roofs: Wikipedia's Joglo and Pendhapa pages don't list roof coverings. Thatch on rural buildings, and shingles or clay tiles on grander ones, is general knowledge here, not from these sources.

### Ornament

Ornament carries prayers and protection as well as beauty: warding off harm (*tolak bala*), asking for good fortune, and showing status. **Plant motifs** are the most common ([GNFI: ragam hias](https://www.goodnewsfromindonesia.id/ragam/budaya/mengenal-ragam-hias-rumah-joglo)).
- **Tlacapan**: a row of triangles, the middle one taller, often at the top or bottom of pillars; plain or filled with stylised leaves and flowers ([ISI journal](https://journal.isi.ac.id/index.php/ars/article/download/4791/2475)).
- **Praba**: a carved relief rising in a high pointed arch, on pillars.
- **Padma**: the lotus form of the umpak.
- The inner tumpang sari is the most richly carved part.

## Meaning

- **Open, without walls**: openness, hospitality and equality. Guests sit *lesehan* on mats, without chairs or tables, level with the host ([GNFI](https://www.goodnewsfromindonesia.id/2025/02/10/rumah-joglo-warisan-arsitektur-jawa-yang-penuh-makna)).
- **A roof like a mountain**: the joglo is said to take its name from a mountain-like (*tajug*) roof, and mountains are sacred. The roof stands for the bond of people, nature and God.
- **Four soko guru** stand for the four directions, the strength of the whole. In Indonesian, *saka guru* has come to mean "a fundamental principle".
- **The sacred centre**: the space beneath the tumpang sari is the most sacred and is often left empty ([Wikipedia: Joglo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joglo)).
  - Incense was burned there weekly for Dewi Sri, the rice goddess (in Central Java also for Nyai Roro Kidul).
  - Brides and grooms sit there at their wedding.
- **The tumpang sari's tiers** are also read as the four stages of the spiritual path: *syariat, tarekat, hakekat, makrifat* (GNFI).
- **Raising the posts**:
  - The umpak are set north-east first, then south-east, north-west and south-west.
  - Each post stands the way its tree grew.
  - An offering ceremony follows in the evening ([Wikipedia: Soko guru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soko_guru)).

### Orientation

- Javanese houses follow a **north-south axis**. One study explains that east-west is avoided because the west (*kulon*) is the seat of Batara Yamadipati, the god who takes life in wayang ([UNY journal](https://journal.uny.ac.id/index.php/humaniora/article/download/5028/4331/12667)).
- In **Yogyakarta, houses face south**, towards the Southern Sea of Nyai Roro Kidul. The layout of a wayang performance in the pringgitan and pendopo shows it: the building faces south ([Adianti](https://www.ejournal.widyamataram.ac.id/index.php/pendapa/article/view/66/52)).
- It is not one rule for all Java. Some houses near Yogyakarta face the palace (east), and regency pendopo generally face the sea ([UNSIQ journal](https://ojs.unsiq.ac.id/index.php/ppkm/article/download/321/155)).

## Our pendopo (Pertapan Jatisrana)

### What it is for in the story

- The pendopo is where the family is whole: Jamadagni on his bale, the five sons on their mats, the oil lamp between them ([environment.md](../environment.md), "Symbols").
- It is where Jamadagni gives the order (beat 2), where the family sits together but apart in the moonlight (S4), and what burns (S5).
- An open pendopo fits the story's meaning: a house that is open to the world is also open to what comes in from it.

- **Posts in their own file** (the user, 2026-10-21: "separate soko in other file"): the teak posts are pieces in `blender/objects/soko.blend` (`scripts/objects/soko.py`), linked as collection instances on their umpak: four soko guru (one each) and four soko penanggap (each used three times, turned as their umpak are).
- **Posts**: two separate collections, every post with its umpak:
  - `Pendopo_SokoGuru`: 4 soko guru, 18 cm square (at ±1.6 m, tops 4.0 m above the floor), on **padma pedestals** 0.72 m tall (= the post's perimeter): a chipped square foot, a plain shaft, a lotus band of two staggered rows of petals, a seat.
  - `Pendopo_SokoPenanggap`: 12 soko penanggap, 13 cm square (on a ring at ±4.7 m, tops 2.45 m above the floor), on plain umpak 0.26 m tall (= half the perimeter), truncated pyramids.
  - Every umpak is a single stone in the **chipped stone style** of the stair: softened wandering edges, chips, lighter worn edges, and on the padma the petal tips lighter, so the carving reads.
- **Objects**: `Pendopo` (plinth, beams, tumpang sari, rafters, battens), `Pendopo_SokoGuru` and `Pendopo_SokoPenanggap` (the posts), `Pendopo_Roof`, `Pendopo_Ridge`; and the linked stone: `Pendopo_UmpakGuru_1..4`, `Pendopo_UmpakPenanggap_1..12`, `Pendopo_Stair`.
- **Beams**: blandar on both post rings. The **sunduk runs through the soko guru**, locked by a **kili** at each end. Tie beams run from the outer ring to the soko guru.
- **Tumpang sari**: 4 tiers on the soko guru. The **outer band steps outward** and carries the rafters; the **inner band steps inward** and forms the stepped ceiling (uleng), each step with a carved lip. A board closes the top, and the **nanasan** hangs from its centre.
- **Roof frame**: rafters (usuk), hip rafters (dudur) on both roofs, and **battens (reng)** across the rafters under the thatch.
- **Roof**: a two-tier alang-alang thatch roof, the steep brunjung overhanging the gentler penanggap (its eave 2.0 m above the floor, 0.8 m past the plinth); a ridge with end ornaments.

The centre beam once called *dhadha peksi* came from memory, not from any source, and was removed: the stepped ceiling closes the centre instead.

### Proposed changes still open

1. **Use the sacred centre in the story**. The empty space under the tumpang sari is where incense burned and where a couple sits at their wedding. Proposal: Jamadagni's incense or lamp stands there in S1, and the centre is cold and empty in S4–S5. This deepens the "warmth is the family" idea.
   - Partly done (2026-10-18): Jamadagni's lamp, the damar ([objects/damar.md](../objects/damar.md)), stands in the sacred centre in S1, between his bale and the sons' mats. In S4 the environment has one of its flames as the only light, so the centre would not be cold then; S5 puts it out. Still to settle with the light states.
2. **Ornament, kept modest** for a hermitage: tlacapan at the tops and feet of the soko guru, a praba on each soko guru, richer carving on the inner tumpang sari. A hermitage is not a palace: plain timber elsewhere.
3. **Roof**: keep two tiers (a simple Joglo Lawakan suits a hermitage), or add a third, gentle emper ring on 20 outer posts for a grander, more classic silhouette. Keeping two tiers is recommended.
4. **The burned version (S5)** for milestone 6: thatch gone or collapsed, the frame charred, the tumpang sari blackened, the soko guru still standing.

### Open questions

- Which of the proposed changes to make, and whether two tiers stay.
- Does a joglo suit a hermitage? Joglo roofs were for nobility; Renuka was a princess, and Jamadagni a great brahmin. A plain joglo seems right for the story, but a limasan pendopo would be the humbler choice.
