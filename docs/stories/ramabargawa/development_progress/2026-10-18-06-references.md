## Done

- `references/README.md`: how to use the guides (your own practice file, appending the script's model to compare), the list of guides, and the external references.
- **01 Modelling the pendopo**: plan, sizes table, and seven parts:
  - set-up
  - plinth and steps
  - umpak and posts (one quadrant, then Mirror)
  - beams, with the sunduk and kili
  - the tumpang sari, its four tiers given as Plane, Inset, Solidify
  - the roof: the penanggap from an inset plane, the brunjung from an extruded plane collapsed to a ridge
  - rafters and battens with Array

  Every step gives what, why, the menu path with the shortcut, and what you'll see. The numbers are the script's (`common/joglo.py`). It ends with pitfalls.
- **02 Texturing with Poly Haven**:
  - the maps and Non-Color; box projection against UV maps
  - the thatch material node by node: Texture Coordinate, Mapping, Image (Box), Mix Color with the palette hex, roughness, Bump
  - a table of all ten project materials with their Poly Haven id, tile size, Mapping scale, palette mix, hex colour and bump strength (from `common/polyhaven.py`)
  - the UV-map route, and pitfalls
- **External reference: [3D Modular STONE FLOOR in Blender, Substance and UE5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sk3ryzQ0Y44)** (brainchildpl, 2022, 51:34).
  - The video has no narration (music only), so it was summarised from one frame every 30 seconds (yt-dlp, 360p, 103 frames) and the on-screen keystrokes. All the downloads were deleted afterwards.
  - The process: sculpt stones, arrange high-poly pieces, model low-poly with UVs, bake, texture in Substance Painter, assemble and light in Unreal.
  - What we can use: chipped stone edges, a modular stone kit for the shrine, gate and steps, baking in Cycles, edge wear from curvature, light setting the mood.
- Memory: read the references before building, and update a guide whenever its script changes.
