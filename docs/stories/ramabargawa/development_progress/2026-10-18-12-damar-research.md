## Found

- **Old Javanese bronze lamps (dipa)**, East Java, 12th–15th century (LACMA, Brooklyn, the National Museum):
  - pole lamps: a head with **four burner dishes** at right angles round a tiered plinth, on a staff; one carries an **ascetic holding a manuscript**
  - hanging lamps with three or four wicks, a kinnara or makara, a meru-shaped top
  - used in worship and household rituals; a lamp purifies a sacred space and stands for enlightenment
- **Clay oil lamps**: among Majapahit's Trowulan pottery; a UGM typology of 9th–10th century lamps (only its bibliography could be read).
- **Blencong**, the wayang lamp: brass Garuda or Jatayu forms, coconut oil, a cotton wick, hung over the dalang; its light read as wisdom and the link between the worlds.
- **Kerosene-era lamps** (senthir, oblek, semprong, teplok, petromak) are too recent for the story.
- Several museum pages could not be opened (403, 429, 503); what came only from search summaries is marked in the doc.
