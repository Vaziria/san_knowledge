## Done

- **A new object file**: `scripts/objects/soko.py` builds `blender/objects/soko.blend` (`run.ps1 object soko`). Its collection `Soko` holds one collection per post, each marked as an **asset**:

  | Piece | What |
  |---|---|
  | `Soko_Guru_1` … `_4` | the soko guru, 0.18 → 0.17 m square, 3.28 m tall |
  | `Soko_Penanggap_1` … `_4` | the soko penanggap, 0.13 → 0.12 m square, 2.19 m tall |

  - Each post stands on z 0 at its own origin, the middle of its foot (where it meets the umpak's top).
  - The posts stand in two rows for review: the soko guru at y 0.6, the soko penanggap in front of the gaps at y −0.6.
  - Each collection's instance offset is its post's foot. The file has its own `_Review` ground, sun and sky.
- **The pendopo links them** (`joglo._linked`, which also links the stone): a post on every umpak.
  - `Pendopo_SokoGuru_1..4` at z 1.32, and `Pendopo_SokoPenanggap_1..12` at z 0.86, in the same two collections as before (`Pendopo_SokoGuru`, `Pendopo_SokoPenanggap`), next to their umpak.
  - The four soko penanggap pieces are used three times each, turned the same way as the umpak under them, so neighbours show different faces of the grain. This is the same modular kit as the stone.
  - The sizes still come from the pakem constants in `joglo.py` (`joglo.soko_pieces`).
- **Code**:
  - `mesh.Parts.add(..., seed=)` gives each post its own grain offset and tone. Before this, the four variants built alone at the same spot would all have had the same grain.
  - `views.py` `SOKO_VIEWS`: all, guru, penanggap, top.
  - `run.ps1` help lists every object file.
