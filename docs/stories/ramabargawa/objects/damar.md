# Damar: the Javanese oil lamp

Jamadagni's oil lamp: the research, then the model (the user, 2026-10-18: "create new model in building. the model is javanese lamp", "do research first", then "do it all"). The Blender file is named after this doc: `blender/objects/damar.blend`.

## What the story needs

From [environment.md](../environment.md), "Symbols in the place":
- The **oil lamp (damar) in the pendapa** belongs to Jamadagni and the household.
- **S1**: lit, between Jamadagni's bale and the five sons' mats.
- **S4**, the moonlit night: the only light, one warm point in the blue-black.
- **S5**: knocked over and out.

The family sits **lesehan**, on mats on the floor ([pendopo.md](../buildings/pendopo.md), "Meaning"), so the flame should be near the eye level of people sitting on the floor, not of people standing.

## Names

- **Damar** is the everyday Javanese word for a lamp; the story docs already use it. (General knowledge; it was not found in a dictionary during this research.)
- **Dipa** (Sanskrit *dīpa*) is the Old Javanese word. A research report on Old Javanese lamps uses it in its title: Timbul Haryono, *Dipa: Bentuk Artefaktual dan Fungsinya dalam Sistem Budaya Masyarakat Jawa Kuna* (UGM, 1989), cited in [Anis 2022](https://etd.repository.ugm.ac.id/home/detail_pencarian_downloadfiles/1021530).
- **Blencong** is the wayang lamp. Its name is thought to come from *cong*, "tilted", for the slanted spout that aims the flame at the screen ([Sonobudoyo](https://sonobudoyo.jogjaprov.go.id/en/tulisan/read/blencong-cahaya-yang-menghidupkan-bayangan-wayang)).

## Kinds of Javanese oil lamp

### Old Javanese bronze lamps (dipa), 12th–15th century

Copper-alloy lamps from East Java, the era of the Hindu-Javanese kingdoms, the world the wayang stories are set in. Two forms survive in museums:

| Lamp | Form | Size |
|---|---|---|
| [Pole Lamp with an Ascetic](https://collections.lacma.org/node/243584), East Java, 14th–15th century (LACMA 82.5) | **A lamp head on a staff**: a cylindrical socket fits a staff or a columnar stand. **Four burner dishes at right angles**, foliate flanges rising from their sides. In the middle, a multitiered square plinth with an **emaciated ascetic holding a manuscript**; flames may rise from his shoulders. LACMA cites a comparable East Javanese pole lamp in the Linden-Museum, Stuttgart (78 212). Note: the page's caption and image text describe a different object, so the record may be mixed up | 15.9 × 12.4 × 10.2 cm |
| [Hanging Lamp with a Kinnara](https://collections.lacma.org/node/174286), East Java, 12th–13th century (LACMA AC1992.271.1) | **Three burner dishes** on a column held by foliate struts. A kinnara (half bird, half human) sits on top; an ovoid frame with a three-lobed loop rises from its back to hang it; a flaring pedestal foot, so it can also stand | 30.5 × 20 × 20 cm |
| A bronze lamp from Java, 13th–15th century (Brooklyn Museum 169257) | A hanging lamp with spouts for **four wicks** and a perch with a **makara**. Known only from search-result summaries; the museum's page could not be opened (rate limit) | not known |
| A Majapahit bronze lamp found in Lamongan (National Museum, Jakarta) | A hanging lamp with a **meru-shaped top**. Known only from a search summary of a [beritajatim](https://beritajatim.com/?p=1037264) article; the page could not be opened | 24 cm tall, 44 cm chain |

What LACMA says about such lamps: they were used in **worship and domestic rituals**; a burning lamp **marks and purifies a sacred space** and can stand for **enlightenment**. Fuel was animal fat or plant oil, lit by a fibre wick. They were hung, set on pedestals or stands, or held in the hand.

An East Javanese bronze style: "the dissolution of all forms into moving curves, volutes and twisted spirals" (an auction description of a Majapahit fitting "for a processional staff or an oil lamp?", via [Hungaricana](https://library.hungaricana.hu/en/view/ArsDecorativa_15_1995/?pg=165) search results; weak source).

### Clay oil lamps

- Majapahit pottery from Trowulan includes **oil lamps**, counted among the "non-container" finds with stoves, figurines and miniature buildings ([Susannawaty, ITB](https://journals.itb.ac.id/index.php/jvad/article/view/694/412), via search results).
- A typology of **Old Javanese oil lamps of the 9th–10th century**, from the collections of Museum Sonobudoyo and the heritage offices of Central Java and Yogyakarta: [Nabila Maharani Anis, UGM 2022](https://etd.repository.ugm.ac.id/home/detail_pencarian_downloadfiles/1021530). Only its bibliography could be downloaded. It cites a study of ritual pottery in Central Javanese temples (Wahyudi 2012) and one on the ornament of Old Javanese bronze lamps in the National Museum (Tingan 2004), which would be the next sources to read.
- A UGM study of Borobudur's reliefs (Karmawibhangga, Lalitavistara, Jataka-Avadana) lists **lamp posts or torch holders** among the landscape elements (via search results; the details were not read).

### Blencong, the wayang lamp

- Brass or bronze, sometimes carved wood. Shapes vary: **Garuda or Jatayu** with spread wings, dragons, the gunungan, flowers; some look like a piggy bank with wings ([Wikipedia id: Blencong](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blencong), [Sonobudoyo](https://sonobudoyo.jogjaprov.go.id/en/tulisan/read/blencong-cahaya-yang-menghidupkan-bayangan-wayang)).
- An oil hole in the middle, the wick in a spout facing the screen, a *sumpit* to pinch the wick.
- **Coconut oil**; the wick is cloth or cotton twisted into a rope, trimmed by the dalang.
- Hung over the dalang's head behind the screen from the **ajon-ajon**; a stage hand, the **penyimping**, tends the flame.
- A late 18th-century brass Garuda blencong is 56.5 × 42 × 54.5 cm ([Roots.sg, Malay Heritage Centre 2019-00573](https://www.roots.gov.sg/Collection-Landing/listing/1466929)).
- Its light is read as **wisdom, enlightenment, and the link between the physical and the spiritual world** (Sonobudoyo). Flickering in the wind, it makes the puppets move on their own (Wikipedia).
- Today it is lit only at the keraton for rituals such as *ruwatan*, and for visitors who want a traditional show.

### Later lamps (not for this story)

Kerosene lamps of the 19th–20th century: **damar oblek / senthir** (a tin with a wick), **dian / semprong** (a glass tube with a rounded foot), **teplok**, **pelita**, **petromak** ([GNFI](https://www.goodnewsfromindonesia.id/indonesiana/humaniora/alat-penerangan-tradisional-penyelamat-saat-mati-lampu), [detik](https://finance.detik.com/berita-ekonomi-bisnis/d-3375805/pria-tamatan-sd-ini-ciptakan-lampu-listrik-unik-menyala-pakai-korek-api)). They belong to modern village life, not to the wayang world of a brahmin's hermitage.

## The model

`project/bargawa/scripts/common/damar.py`, built into `blender/objects/damar.blend` by `scripts/objects/damar.py` (`run.ps1 object damar`). Stylized and simplified; the foot's underside at the origin, the ascetic facing south. Total height 0.89 m (the aureole's tip); the flames' roots at 0.782 m, their tips about 0.82 m above the floor.

| Shaft | a turned post: a bulb near the foot, two rings, a collar at the top | 0.036–0.06 thick, to 0.70 | teak, turned and oiled |
| Socket | a bronze sleeve over the shaft's top, with a lip | 0.067 across at the lip, 0.675 → 0.75 | bronze |

| Flames | four, one at each wick's tip, each an instance of the flame piece; they sway, breathe and lick with time, each in its own way | 0.034 tall, 0.012 wide | drawn by their shader on three crossed cards: pale gold inside, an orange rim, a touch of blue at the root |

**The flame is a piece of its own** (`Damar_Flame`): the flame cards and a small warm point light (0.5 W, radius 2 cm, flickering about ±12%), its root at the instance's origin. The lamp `Damar` places four of them, all lit, as in S1. For S4, a light state can link the lamp and place one flame on one wick; `damar.FLAMES` gives the four wick tips.

**The flame follows Max Hay's fire video** ([references/video-fire-max-hay.md](../../../../project/bargawa/references/video-fire-max-hay.md); the user, 2026-10-18: "i have new reference for creating fire"). Code: `scripts/common/fire.py`.
- **Fire on cards.** The video puts a fire video on a plane. With no stock video (sources are Blender and Poly Haven), the shader draws the "footage" instead, on three upright cards through the flame's axis, so it reads from any side.
- **Alpha keyed from its brightness**, the black handle raised to cut the faint edge, as in the video.
- **An orange multiplied in** (`Flame_Tint`), so the light states can change the flame's colour.
- **Time from the frame** (a driver, `frame / 24`). Each instance starts at its own moment (Object Info › Random), as the video offsets copies of its footage.
- **A point light the flame's size** does the lighting, as in the video; the emission stays low.
- **Kept different**: Multiple Importance stays on. His 0.6 m light showed as a ball in the water; ours is the flame's size, so its glint on the bronze is the flame's own.
- **The video's other tools**: Volume Scatter haze and sparks belong to the light states and to the pyre (S6).

**Materials:**
- **Bronze** (`textures.bronze`):
  - a bronze colour, with olive patina gathered in the hollows (an Ambient Occlusion node, 1.2 cm) and in faint patches (noise)
  - bright polished bronze where the `wear` attribute is high: the dish rims, the socket's lip, the tiers' edges, the flange tips, the ascetic's knees and the crown of his head, where hands and cloths rub
  - patina rougher and less metallic than the metal
- **Turned teak** (`teak_turned`): the teak texture through grain UVs, finer and darker, without the weathering layers of the pendopo's timber. On a 2 cm turned stand every face read as a worn edge, and the dirt rising from the foot covered all of it. A household thing is handled and oiled.
- **Flame** (`fire.flame_material`): an emission strength of 1.6, mixed with transparency by its alpha. Brighter values read white.

**Placed** in the hermitage by `common/dressing.py` (`LAMP`): a linked instance of `Damar` at (0, 1.3) from the pendopo's centre, on the floor. The placeholder (a clay dish on a pole by the bale) is gone.

**Lessons from the checks** (`renders/checks/damar/`):
- A flame mesh around its own light hides that light: the flame must not cast shadows (`visible_shadow = False`).
- At 1.5 W and a centimetre away, the lights turned the whole head copper-pink in daylight. 0.5 W with a 2 cm radius keeps the day look and still lights the head at night.
- Tubes with 6 or 8 sides read as flat bands under the 40° sharp-edge shading: the arms, struts and wicks need 10–12.
- An aureole with tongues pointing straight out read as a stepped tower; the tongues must curl up.
- Leaf flanges of 5 points read as flat diamonds; curved strips read as leaves.
- **The flame on cards**:
  - The width curve must not run on past its ends; it lit a solid band above the tip.
  - A card seen edge-on showed as a line through the flame; it now fades with the viewing angle.
  - Alpha from the shape alone outlined the flame in red; keyed from the brightness, the rim fades.
  - From straight above, the flame is nearly gone (the video's warning).
