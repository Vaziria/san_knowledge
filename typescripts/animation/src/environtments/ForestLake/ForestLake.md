A valley round a lake, as the reference's overview lays it out, looking north from the south shore:

1. **The lake** in the middle, about 52 m across east to west and 43 m north to south (widened on 2026-10-01, task 21: its west shore 6 m further west and its east shore 6 m further east, with the pier, the boat, the camp, the ruins, the wreck, the outlet, the bridge, the stream, the east pier, the cave, the eastern cliffs, the watch tower and the heights path moved out with them; the landing, the south shore before it and the north under the waterfalls stayed), clear turquoise in the shallows over a sandy bed, deepening to blue, 3 m deep at most. Bright lines of light wander over it in cells, the sky shows in it at a glance, the sun glints off its small waves, and white foam gathers at its shore and round the rocks in it. A ring of boulders lines its shore, their feet in the water. Lily pads with pink water lilies float in its shallows, and reeds with brown cattails stand in them.
2. **Two waterfalls** from the cliffs to the north: the main one about 10 m high, from a river running over the heights, and a smaller one, about 6 m, to the northwest. Each is a falling sheet of blue-white water, blue and cyan streaks running down it, white only on its brightest streaks, along its edges, where it tips over and where it lands (tasks/lighting.md: it drew the eye when it was mostly white), with white foam heaving where it lands, mist rising and boulders standing in its pool. A wooden footbridge crosses the main river just above its fall.
3. **The island**, off the middle of the lake toward its east shore: a plinth of rock about 17 m across (grown 1.7 times on 2026-10-01, task 21, north and east, so the open water north of the landing stays open), rising steeply out of deep water, ringed with boulders, with a level grassy top. On it **the great crystal tree**, the tree that guards the forest: the user's own [crystal tree](../../figures/Tree/CrystalTree.md), grown from its seed to 22 m, the tallest thing in the valley, its crown about as wide, reaching out over the water on every side. Its S-curved trunk and five roots are massive, the roots gripping the island's top and running down over its rock toward the water, crystals growing from the ground and the rock round them. Its crystals are its page's own blue and violet, the same all year, glowing in every season and lighting the tree and the island round it; crystals hang from its outer clusters on strings, swinging, and sparkles drift round its whole crown. Its bark takes the forest lake's colours, and in winter snow lies on its trunk's, roots' and branches' upturned faces. Round it, between its roots: a bush and flowers, a double lamp (two lanterns on a crossbar) either side of the tree, a sign post by the steps, and a fence round its top on two sides, a gap in it where a root passes; stone steps lead up from its jetty.
4. **The piers**: one from the southwest shore with a lamp at its landward corner and a rowboat moored beside it, a shorter one from the east shore, and the island's jetty.
5. **The outlet**: the lake runs out to the southeast in a stream that drops 1.9 m down a cascade just under an arched wooden bridge, then winds away southeast between rocky banks.
6. **The forest paths**: the paths of `PATHS` in [layout.ts](layout.ts), from the camp along the south shore, past the landing, over the bridge and north along the east shore to the cave, with fences along stretches of them, lamp posts along them, pebbles here and there, and sign posts where paths meet (some with a lantern). Branches run from the camp north to the ruins and to the pier, one runs along the heights from the watch tower, and the way in comes from the south through **the forest entrance**, a wooden gate with a lantern hanging each side, fences running off from its posts. Each path changes as it goes: a run of stretches 8–30 m long, no two neighbours alike, each of a kind that suits where it goes (`PATH_KINDS` in [Paths.ts](Paths.ts), one table):
   - the main path, from the camp past the landing to the bridge: dirty stone by the camp, regular stone, sandy dirt, regular stone again;
   - on from the bridge along the east shore to the cave: mossy stone, then dirt in the forest's shade;
   - from the camp to the ruins: ruined stone, more broken the nearer it gets, then gravel;
   - the camp's spur to the pier: dirt worn into two tracks, grass up the middle;
   - along the heights from the watch tower: stepping stones, a stride apart in the grass, then gravel, pebbles packed in dirt with loose ones spilling onto the grass;
   - the way in from the gate: regular stone, two tracks under the trees, regular stone.

   The stone is the [stone path](../../figures/ForestLake/StonePath/StonePath.md)'s variations, flat, chunky flagstones over sandy dirt. Wherever a path climbs more steeply than 1 in 5 it is steps: stone stairs on a path 2 m wide or more (by the east shore), log steps on a narrower one, earth held level behind a log across the path at each step (on the ruins' branch and on the heights); where the heights path runs down the cliff over the cave, nothing is laid. Where two kinds meet they blend over about 2 m: a stone path breaks up into single stones on the dirt, then dirt.

   The paths' lines stay where `PATHS` puts them, so the fences, posts and gate still stand by them. Each path is up to 30% wider or narrower along its way: wider where paths meet and by the camp, the landing, the gate and the sign posts, narrower in the forest and on the heights, and never within 0.3 m of a post. Its edges are uneven, the grass reaching in and bare earth reaching out, a tuft now and then in the path. Along the way, in their season: roots across the forest stretches, puddles in dips (ice in winter), moss along the shaded edges, a few stones kicked onto the grass; in autumn leaves drifted along the edges; in winter a trodden track down the middle of the snow, with footprints.

   **Two trails** (`TRAILS` in [Paths.ts](Paths.ts)), 0.7–0.8 m wide, go where no path did: **the wreck trail**, trodden dirt from the main path's end by the camp, east of the camp's fence, then west between the wreck's debris to its ramp; and **the heights trail**, stepping stones from the watch tower's path round the tower's east side and over the heights to the footbridge over the main river, meeting its deck between the rails. What stood on a trail is taken out once the forest is placed (trees, bushes and rocks within 1.5 m of its middle, grass and flowers on it), so nothing else moves.

   Walking: the stairs' and the log steps' treads are ground; nothing else on the paths but soft plants rises 6 cm over it.
7. **The camp**, in the southwest: a trodden clearing with a tent facing a campfire, a pot hanging over the fire from a tripod, log seats round it, crates and barrels by the tent, a lamp at its way in and a fence round its back.
8. **The cave**, in the cliffs to the east, as big as the reference draws it: its mouth glowing orange, torches either side, a campfire before it.
9. **The ruins**, on a terrace to the west: a stone arch, broken pillars and fallen blocks.
10. **The watch tower**, on the heights to the northeast, its flag waving.
11. **The spaceship wreck** (added 2026-10-01 from the user's seven reference sheets of it), in a clearing southwest of the camp, just behind its fence: a ship crashed and broken into its modules ([SpaceshipWreck.md](../../figures/ForestLake/SpaceshipWreck/SpaceshipWreck.md)), its nose east and its torn-open hold toward the camp, lying on ground it levelled and churned to bare earth, with a furrow it ploughed coming in from the west behind its tail, the forest cleared round it.

Round all of it: cliffs of tall, faceted grey columns of rock under the heights to the north and east, capped with moss, with bushes and grass on their ledges and along their top, and boulders at their feet. A mixed forest stands on every side, pines, round broadleaf trees and cherries, thick right down to the lake's banks and sparser on the far hills. Its trees are of three ages (task 21): young ones, about one in six, 2–4 m (cherries 3–4 m, broadleaf trees 3–4.5 m), slender, where light reaches (at the forest's edges and clearings, along the paths and by the shore); grown ones, most of them, 6–12 m; and old ones, about one in ten, 13–16 m (cherries 7–8 m, broadleaf trees 9.5–11 m), broad in trunk and crown, deep in the forest and on the heights, standing up out of the forest's top, so its top is uneven. Bushes grow at the forest's edges, tall grass in clumps and flowers in the meadows, rocks lie along the shore and the stream, and logs, stumps and mushrooms in the forest. Beyond are forested hills and snowy mountains, tallest to the north, under a bright blue sky with big white clouds drifting, all hazed with distance.

## Seasons

The same valley in each, as the reference's four seasons show it; everything in it takes the season (every asset has a `season` option):

- **Spring**: fresh green grass, cherry trees in pink blossom, pink water lilies, flowers of every colour in the meadows; cherry petals fall round the trees by the landing; on the island the crystal tree's sparkles drift instead, in every season.
- **Summer**: a deeper, denser green; the cherry trees in green leaf; white water lilies; white and yellow flowers.
- **Autumn**: the broadleaf trees orange, red and gold, the cherry trees red, some pines golden larches, the grass gold; fallen leaves lie thick on the ground and float on the lake by its shore, and leaves fall from the trees by the landing; the tent's canvas orange; more mushrooms; a warmer haze.
- **Winter**: snow on everything, the grass, the trees' tiers and branches, the rocks, the cliffs' ledges, the roofs and decks; icicles on the cliffs' top, the watch tower's eaves and over the cave's mouth; bare trees among the pines; red berries on the bushes and in sprigs out of the snow; the lake frozen, plates of pale ice with white cracks between them, but for a hole of open water where a floating figure goes; the rivers ice; the waterfalls nearly still; snow falling; a paler sky.

Each season brings its own light: a warm sun and a sky light pale blue from above and, from below, the ground's colour, softened (a little green off the grass, gold off autumn's leaves, white off the snow). Colours stay vivid in the shade, as the reference paints them. One sun leads (tasks/lighting.md): the sky light is about a third of the sun, so the shadows are deep and what the sun reaches stands out; the ground is darker in the trees' shadows and round the foot of every trunk, rock and bush, and its grass is patched lighter and darker; and a thin haze from near at hand lightens what is far toward the sky's colour, so the cliffs and the far forest stand back.

Figures stand at the landing, a level clearing on the south shore, looking north across the lake to the island and the main waterfall; a figure in the water (the boat, the fish) goes to open water west of the island (in winter the hole in the ice). The views from the landing, north to the water and south behind the preview cameras, are kept clear of trees and bushes.

The walking camera walks the land, the water's surface (and its ice), the piers' and bridges' decks, the island's steps, the cave's floor and the wreck's ramp and hold, within 75 m of the lake's middle.

How it moves: the water's waves, light and foam; the waterfalls' streaks, foam and mist; the stream running; the flames and sparks of the campfires and torches; the lanterns flickering; the flag waving; the wreck's navigation light blinking, its engine's core glowing brighter and dimmer and a lamp flickering in its hold; the clouds drifting; the petals, leaves or snow falling; the moored boat riding the waves (frozen still in winter). No day and night, no wind.

Size: the valley about 120 m across inside its forest; land out to 340 m, hills and mountains to 1.1 km.
