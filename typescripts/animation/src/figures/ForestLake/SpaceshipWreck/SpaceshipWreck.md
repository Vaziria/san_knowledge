A ship crashed and broken into its modules, lying in a clearing it churned up, grown over a little. From its nose to its tail:

1. **The cockpit**, broken off the hull and turned aside, its nose dug into the ground: a pointed, faceted nose of plates, orange low down toward its tip; its canopy torn open over the seats, the frame left round the gap, a few shards of glass in it; two antennas on its back. Inside, lit by its screens and a lamp: a floor, two pilots' seats side by side facing the nose, a console across in front with three glowing screens, a stick before each seat, a side console and a storage box. Ragged where it broke off.
2. **The hull's front module**: a tube of plates, torn open at its front where the cockpit broke off, plates bent out round the tear; looking in, a dark hold, its ribs, and a bulkhead with a shut door.
3. **The middle module**, its side and top torn wide open toward the front (+z), as the hull sheet's cutaway: the ceiling's frames still across the gap; a floor of plates with a grate down it; bulkheads with doorways at both ends; pipes low on the far wall and a cable tray under its ceiling; a console with two screens; three lamps glowing warm (one flickers); the ship's cargo boxes and the explorer's wooden crates. Its ramp is let down from the gap's foot onto the ground.
4. **The rear module**, whole but for a few holes; the right wing still on it, drooping to the ground, and the tail on its top.
5. **The engine**, its fan toward the back: a round casing of plates with dark rings at its ends and a cooling pipe along each upper side; a thick intake ring round the duct; a fan of eighteen twisted blades on a hub; behind it the stator's vanes and the core, still glowing a dull orange, slowly brighter and dimmer.
6. **The tail**: a fairing of plates along the hull's top, and on it the tall vertical fin, swept back, orange along its leading edge and up its front and pale behind; an antenna mast beside it; a navigation light at the fin's tip, blinking orange.
7. **The wings**: flat, swept, tapering slabs, plated on both faces, pale with orange tips and orange along the leading edge toward the tip, a flap along the trailing edge hanging half torn off its hinge. The right one is still on the hull; the left one lies torn off on the ground in front, its root ragged, its spars sticking out and a cable hanging.
8. **The debris** round it: a torn piece of the hull lying on the ground by the cockpit; plates lying about and two leaning on the hull; the ship's cargo boxes (dark grey, orange straps) and the explorer's wooden crates by the ramp; three red fuel drums; cables from the hull onto the ground and one in a coil; pipes; small parts; a pilot's seat thrown clear on its side; an antenna mast stood up by the nose; rocks the crash pushed up, most at its nose; and what has grown back: tall grass along the hull's foot, moss, flowers, a few bushes and two dead trees.

Every plate is a chunky slab over the dark frame, which shows between the plates and wherever one is gone; a hole leaves a spar and a rib across it, a plate torn up hangs off an edge it still holds to, and wherever the wall ends its thickness shows. Most plates are warm off-white, some dark grey, orange ones in patches, a few mossy on top; all spotted with rust, their corners now and then rusted over; square portholes and vents here and there. The insides are lit warm by their lamps.

Low poly, every face flat shaded, bright and saturated, as the sheets are.

Size: about 24 m long, 18 m across its wings and what lies round it, the fin's tip 8 m up (the sheets' "Overall Size": length 24 m, width 18 m, height 8 m). The hull is about 4 m round, the engine 4.4 m.

Seasons: In autumn leaves lie among the debris and the grass is gold; in winter snow lies on every upturned face of it, the hold and the cockpit still lit inside.
